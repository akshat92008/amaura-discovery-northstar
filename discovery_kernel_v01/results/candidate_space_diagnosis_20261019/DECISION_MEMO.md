## 1. Ranked Diagnosis of Bottlenecks (Phase 5)

Based on empirical recalculation, prompt inspection, and comparison against historical experiments (Stage 3 Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Benchmark v1 120B Nemotron), the six potential explanations are ranked by the weight of direct evidence:

### Rank 1: Poor Feedback and Exploration Policy (Search Controller Failure)
- **Supporting Evidence:**
  - Arm B generated 10/12 exact duplicates of the few-shot prompt example (`4e970cdd`) and 2/12 duplicates of `ab896d87`.
  - 100% of Arm B proposals collapsed into **a single behavioral algorithm** on development instances (mean $C_{\max} = 3654.15$).
  - Arm A (independent search with zero feedback) explored 7 unique recipes and 6 distinct behaviors, finding a candidate that scored 3587.75 (66 units better than Arm B).
  - Feedback directly caused a **-2.151% performance degradation** ($p < 0.001$).
  - The controller provided zero novelty pressure, zero duplicate filtering, and reinforced the prompt attractor in every round.
- **Contradicting Evidence:** Arm A also had 5 duplicates out of 12, indicating base model bias contributes to repetition even without feedback.
- **Confidence:** **HIGH**.
- **What would change assessment:** If an experiment with an explicit tabu list and novelty penalty still produced mode collapse across diverse prompts.

---

### Rank 2: Weak Problem-Solving Capability of the Chosen Model (`qwen2.5-coder:1.5b`)
- **Supporting Evidence:**
  - The 1.54B parameter model exhibits strong in-context repetition bias and failed to parse the semantic directive to "refine or improve" into an exploratory mutation.
  - Rationales generated by the model largely parroted the text of the system prompt rather than demonstrating causal hypothesis generation.
- **Contradicting Evidence:**
  - The model achieved 100% syntactic compliance (24/24 valid JSON configs).
  - Prior experiments with frontier reasoning models (120B Nemotron-3 Super in Benchmark v1) also exhibited an 84.1% collapse into monotonic equivalence traps when prompted in conversational loops. Model scale alone has historically failed to solve this in our repository.
- **Confidence:** **HIGH**.
- **What would change assessment:** If a 70B+ reasoning model given the exact same prompt structure successfully generated diverse, non-repeating candidates across rounds without explicit controller constraints.

---

### Rank 3: Restricted or Poorly Structured Candidate Representation
- **Supporting Evidence:**
  - The declarative space is a Cartesian product of classical components (NEH, Palmer, Taillard insertion) and cannot express state-of-the-art PFSP metaheuristics (Iterated Greedy loops, ruin-and-recreate, simulated annealing).
  - Several combinations are mathematically degenerate (e.g. `makespan_only` + `flow_time_min` is identical to `makespan_and_flow_time`).
  - Budget caps ($\le 500$) truncate search prematurely, masking the difference between single-pass and multi-pass neighborhoods on instances $n \ge 30$.
- **Contradicting Evidence:**
  - The representation **already contained the winning baseline** (`neh_ls_insertion`), yet the AI search failed to locate it.
  - Arm A found a configuration (`arm_a_prop_08`) that beat classical NEH and came within 0.37% of NEH-LS at a 3.3x speedup. The space was expressive enough to discover a valid Pareto configuration; the search controller simply failed to navigate it.
- **Confidence:** **MEDIUM**.
- **What would change assessment:** If a flawless search controller completely exhausted the 780 configurations and demonstrated that no configuration in the space could beat the baseline.

---

### Rank 4: Candidate Selection or Objective-Design Problems
- **Supporting Evidence:**
  - Selection was driven by unweighted mean makespan across heterogeneous instance sizes (small, medium, large). Large instances contribute disproportionate absolute variance, biassing selection toward large-instance behavior.
- **Contradicting Evidence:**
  - The ranking was stable and consistent: `neh_ls_insertion` was #1 on both dev and holdout; `arm_a_prop_08` was #2 on both dev and holdout; `arm_b_prop_01` was last on both dev and holdout. Selection mechanics did not cause the observed failure.
- **Confidence:** **MEDIUM**.
- **What would change assessment:** If instance-normalized gap metrics ($\Delta C_{\max} / \text{LB}$) produced a different champion than unweighted mean makespan.

---

### Rank 5: Genuine Lack of Advantage on PFSP
- **Supporting Evidence:**
  - Constructive heuristics for PFSP have been intensely refined for 40 years. Within simple constructive/insertion heuristics, beating NEH-LS by $\ge 1.0\%$ across diverse Taillard dimensions may be mathematically impossible without iterative metaheuristics.
- **Contradicting Evidence:**
  - Metaheuristic variations (Iterated Greedy) comfortably beat NEH-LS by $> 1.0\%$, showing that the problem domain itself permits discovery if iterative control structures are unlocked.
- **Confidence:** **MEDIUM**.
- **What would change assessment:** Mathematical proof that no sequential permutation heuristic can improve makespan over NEH-LS by $\ge 1.0\%$ without stochastic restart.

---

### Rank 6: Insufficient Evaluation Data or Experimental Power
- **Supporting Evidence:** None.
- **Contradicting Evidence:**
  - The sample size of 20 dev and 30 holdout instances produced tight bootstrap confidence intervals (e.g. $[-2.625\%, -1.658\%]$ for Arm B vs Arm A), conclusively rejecting the discovery hypothesis with $p < 0.001$.
- **Confidence:** **LOW** (Sample size is definitely NOT the bottleneck).

## 2. Selection of Primary Next Direction (Phase 6)

### **Selected Direction: B. Improve the Search Controller**

### 2.1 Why Option B is the Primary Bottleneck
We must not improve representation (A) or change models (C) before fixing the search controller, because:
1. **The Representation Was Not the Blocking Ceiling:** The winning baseline (`neh_ls_insertion`) was already expressible in the declarative DSL. The system failed not because the space lacked a winning point, but because the search controller visited only 1 distinct point in Arm B and only 6 in Arm A.
2. **Representation Expansion Will Exacerbate Mode Collapse:** If we expand the representation to include complex metaheuristic operators (Option A) while retaining the current conversational controller, the LLM will still anchor on whatever example is in the prompt and repeat it endlessly.
3. **Model Scaling is Wasteful on a Broken Controller:** Historical evidence in this repository (`LIVE_PILOT_REPORT.md` with 120B Nemotron) proves that scaling to 120B parameters does not prevent mode collapse when prompts rely on open-ended conversational feedback. Paying for frontier API tokens to run a broken search loop is irresponsible engineering.

### 2.2 Concrete Architecture of the Improved Search Controller
The next iteration of North BlueStar must replace conversational prompt-looping with **Hybrid Evolutionary-LLM Optimization**:
1. **Tabu & Duplicate Rejection Gate:**
   - Maintain an append-only registry of evaluated canonical `recipe_hash`es.
   - If an LLM proposal duplicates an existing recipe, immediately reject it at generation time (zero compute spent on evaluation) and prompt the model with an explicit taboo constraint.
2. **Demonstration Neutrality:**
   - Remove the concrete, fully formed recipe from `SCHEMA_EXAMPLE`.
   - Provide only abstract syntax (e.g. `<field_name>: <allowed_option>`) to eliminate the primary attractor.
3. **Explicit Mutation & Crossover Operators:**
   - Instead of asking the model to "improve based on evidence" in open prose, formulate the query as a structured mutation:
     *"Given parent recipe A (score X), mutate exactly 1 component (e.g. initial_order or local_search) to explore an alternative hypothesis."*
   - Implement programmatic crossover: take the constructive heuristic of Champion A and the local search neighborhood of Champion B.

## 3. What Should NOT Be Built Yet

To maintain scientific discipline and avoid another experimental treadmill:
1. **Do NOT run larger LLM candidate-generation campaigns** (e.g. 50 or 100 proposals with 1.5B or 70B models) until the search controller has programmatic duplicate rejection.
2. **Do NOT rewrite or expand the PFSP candidate space** into unconstrained Python execution. Process safety and declarative containment are working perfectly; they are not the problem.
3. **Do NOT switch problem domains.** Switching domains merely imports the same search controller failure into a new setting. Fix the search mechanics where we have pristine baseline data and fast evaluators.
4. **Do NOT launch paid API calls.** The failure modes can be entirely diagnosed, fixed, and verified using local zero-cost inference.
